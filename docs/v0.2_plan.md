# SILENT MEDIC VET — v0.2 plan and product roadmap

Prepared 2026-09-06 · Jae H. Choi, MSc, PhD, DVSc · Auravyx Systems
Baseline: v0.1.0, 64 entries × 13 species, 6 tiers, 11 problem flows.

## 1. Where v0.1 stands

The knowledge base is structurally sound (tiers, 3C red band, 21 CFR 530.41 card, food-animal withdrawal fields all present) but it is a companion-animal-and-horse formulary with ruminant and swine cells bolted on. Of 64 entries, only seven carry a ruminant or swine label indication (fenbendazole, ivermectin, amprolium, flunixin, oxytocin, rbST, Improvest). None of the periparturient metabolic diseases, neonatal diseases, or herd-level parasite programs that make up most of food-animal practice are represented, and there is no problem flow for bloat, ketosis, hypocalcemia, mastitis, neonatal scours, footrot, or piglet anemia.

Verification status: 3 of 64 entries carry PMIDs. The remaining 61 are label- or formulary-derived with no recorded citation. The review workbook (`docs/dose_review_v0.1.xlsx`) enumerates all 832 species cells; 210 cells are food-animal cells lacking a withdrawal string, which violates the "uncertain → FARAD" rule and must be fixed in v0.2 regardless of anything else.

## 2. Product direction (from the 2026-09-06 decision)

Target: a clinical decision-support product sold to veterinarians, covering parasitology, infectious disease, metabolic/management disease, and therapeutics for all 13 species, with an owner mode as the free tier. That changes three things about how the KB is built.

Content standard. Every disease and drug card must be traceable to a named source: an approved product label (NADA/ANADA number), Plumb's Veterinary Drug Handbook (edition), a FARAD digest, a current textbook, or a PubMed-indexed paper with PMID. Textbook anchors I will work from (cited, never copied): Constable et al., *Veterinary Medicine* 11th ed.; Smith, *Large Animal Internal Medicine* 6th ed.; Pugh & Baird, *Sheep, Goat, and Cervid Medicine* 3rd ed.; Zimmerman et al., *Diseases of Swine* 11th ed.; Ettinger, *Textbook of Veterinary Internal Medicine* 9th ed.; Taylor, Coop & Wall, *Veterinary Parasitology* 4th ed.; Bowman, *Georgis' Parasitology for Veterinarians* 11th ed.; Quesenberry & Carpenter, *Ferrets, Rabbits, and Rodents* 4th ed.; Swayne, *Diseases of Poultry* 14th ed.; and the Merck Veterinary Manual online for current-status checks. All text in the app is original synthesis; no passage is reproduced from a copyrighted source.

Schema. A `disease` record type is added alongside the existing drug/treatment record, with fields for species, etiology, signalment, clinical signs, differentials, diagnostics, treatment (linking to drug entry IDs), prevention/management, zoonotic and reportable status, and references. Existing 64 entries and their fields are untouched.

Maintenance. The KB gets a standing review cycle (section 6) so the product stays current after release.

## 3. v0.2 scope — Ruminant/Swine expansion

### 3a. New treatment entries (target 26; 64 → 90)

Rx status reflects GFI #263 (June 2023): all medically important antimicrobials are prescription-only, so injectable oxytetracycline, penicillin G, and sulfonamides go in Tier 2, not Tier 1. Ionophores and anthelmintics remain OTC. Each entry is added only after the label (DailyMed / FDA Animal Drugs@FDA) and Plumb's dose are read for that species; withdrawal strings come from the label or are written as "FARAD".

Tier 1 · OTC
1. Albendazole (Valbazen) — cattle, sheep, goats; liver fluke; teratogenic first 45 d of gestation (X flag for early pregnancy).
2. Levamisole (Prohibit) — cattle, sheep; narrow margin in goats, toxicosis signs.
3. Morantel (Rumatel) — goats and cattle, 0-d milk withdrawal; one of few goat-labeled dewormers.
4. Moxidectin (Cydectin) — cattle pour-on/injectable, sheep drench; goat extra-label.
5. Decoquinate (Deccox) — coccidiosis prevention calves, lambs, kids.
6. Monensin / lasalocid (Rumensin, Bovatec) — ionophore coccidiostat and feed efficiency; X card: fatal in horses, toxic in dogs, sheep sensitive to monensin.
7. Copper oxide wire particles — Haemonchus control in sheep/goats; evidence: Vatta 2012, PMID 22789299; Burke 2011, PMID 22192772; Kearney 2016 review, PMID 26995728.
8. Poloxalene (Bloat Guard) — frothy bloat, cattle.
9. Propylene glycol drench — ketosis (cattle), pregnancy toxemia (sheep/goats); evidence: McArt 2012, PMID 22541477 (RCT, 741 cows).
10. Oral calcium bolus — postpartum hypocalcemia, cattle; evidence: Martinez 2016, PMID 27423947 and 27423945 (450-cow RCT; note harm signal in primiparous cows — this goes in the DVM note).
11. Iron dextran injection — piglet iron-deficiency anemia, 200 mg IM day 1–3; evidence: Starzyński 2013, PMID 23737963; Pierce 2024, PMID 38219027 (second dose).
12. Zinc sulfate footbath — footrot, sheep/goats/cattle.
13. Bismuth subsalicylate / kaolin-pectin — calves, kids, lambs GI support (FARAD withdrawal).

Tier 2 · Rx
14. Oxytetracycline LA — cattle, swine, sheep labels; pinkeye, footrot, BRD, anaplasmosis.
15. Penicillin G procaine — label dose vs. FARAD extended-withdrawal reality; sheep/goat extra-label.
16. Ceftiofur (Naxcel / Excede / Excenel) — cattle, swine, sheep, goats; 2012 cephalosporin extra-label order restrictions.
17. Tulathromycin (Draxxin) — cattle BRD, footrot, IBK; swine; no US sheep/goat label.
18. Florfenicol (Nuflor) — cattle, swine; not in lactating dairy; goat extra-label.
19. Tilmicosin (Micotil) — cattle, sheep; X card: fatal to humans on self-injection, fatal to pigs and horses, goat deaths reported.
20. Sulfadimethoxine (Albon) — calves, poultry; prohibited extra-label in lactating dairy.
21. Thiamine — polioencephalomalacia, ruminants; evidence: Apley 2015 review, PMID 25705027 (no RCT exists; graded B on physiology and label).
22. Calcium borogluconate IV / magnesium — milk fever, grass tetany; small-ruminant periparturient review: Brozos 2011, PMID 21215894.
23. Dexamethasone — ketosis adjunct, PEM adjunct, induced parturition/abortion warning.
24. Xylazine / tolazoline — ruminants need one-tenth the equine dose; not for food animals without FARAD.
25. Lidocaine local blocks (cornual, caudal epidural, ring block) — with meloxicam extra-label analgesia; evidence: Heinrich 2010, PMID 20494153 (calf dehorning); Keita 2010, PMID 20636569 (piglet castration).

Tier 3A · Approved biologic
26. Gonadorelin (Cystorelin / Factrel) — cystic ovarian disease, Ovsynch; cattle.

Also in v0.2: expand existing Meloxicam entry with cattle/swine extra-label analgesia notes (same PMIDs as item 25), and Flunixin with the "IV only in cattle; IM causes violative residues" note.

Deferred to v0.3: vaccines (CD&T, tetanus antitoxin, BRD, erysipelas, PRRS), altrenogest, prostaglandin F2α, colostrum/FPT management (Godden 2019, PMID 31590901), FAMACHA as a Tier 6 manual entry (Kaplan 2004, PMID 15265575).

### 3b. New problem flows in template.html (7)

bloat · ketosis / pregnancy toxemia · hypocalcemia / grass tetany · mastitis · neonatal scours (calf, lamb, kid, piglet) · footrot / ruminant lameness branch · piglet failure-to-thrive (anemia, iron, crushing, starvation). Existing 11 flows are not modified except to add ruminant/swine branches inside `lame`, `gi`, and `neuro` (PEM, listeriosis, tetanus, grass tetany).

### 3c. Withdrawal gap closure

All 210 food-animal cells lacking a withdrawal string receive "FARAD" (or the label value where one exists). Tier 4–6 entries that are non-drug (cold therapy, weight management, exercise, farriery) receive "n/a — no drug administered" so the field is never blank.

### 3d. Verification protocol for every new entry

1. Label: DailyMed or Animal Drugs@FDA — dose, species, withdrawal, NADA. 2. Plumb's — extra-label dose range. 3. FARAD — withdrawal when extra-label. 4. PubMed — at least one PMID for any efficacy or safety claim beyond the label; PMID confirmed by metadata fetch before entry. 5. Reviewer sign-off in `dose_review` before the entry ships.

## 3e. Prescribing module (added 2026-09-07)

Three phases. Phase 1 shipped as v0.1.2: eight DVM reference cards (VCPR, prescription elements and refills, dispensed-drug label, AMDUCA conditions and records, controlled substances under DEA and Texas law, compounding under GFI #256, VFD and GFI #263, prescription portability and online pharmacies), each verified against eCFR, FDA, TBVME and AVMA text. Phase 2 (v0.3): condition-based prescribing protocols — first- and second-line drug, dose link, duration, monitoring — built on ISCAID, AAHA/AAFP, ACVIM consensus statements and BSAVA/WSAVA guidance for companion animals, and AABP/AASV/AASRP guidance for food animals. Phase 3 (v0.6 DVM tools): prescription writer — species, weight, product concentration → quantity, mL or tablets per dose, withdrawal date, and a label text block that meets 573.40 and 530.12.

## 4. Roadmap beyond v0.2

v0.3 — Parasitology module: 13-species parasite cards (life cycle, diagnosis, drug links, resistance status, refugia/FAMACHA programs), fecal egg count interpretation, small-animal heartworm/flea/tick protocols.
v0.4 — Infectious disease module: ~120 disease cards; reportable/zoonotic flags per USDA list; poultry (Marek's, coccidiosis, mycoplasma, avian influenza reporting), swine (PRRS, erysipelas, PED), ruminant (BRD, Johne's, CL, listeriosis, CAE/OPP), companion (parvo, panleuk, lepto, RHDV2).
v0.5 — Herd/flock management: vaccination schedules, biosecurity, nutrition, VFD workflow, withdrawal calculator with date arithmetic.
v0.6 — DVM tools: mg/kg → mL calculator per concentration, CRI calculator, fluid plan, body-weight estimator.
v1.0 — Commercial release: paid DVM tier (license key, offline validation), free owner tier, full review sign-off on every card, terms of use, professional liability review.

Business and legal items to settle before v1.0 (flagged, not decided here): product liability and the veterinarian-only disclaimer wording; state VCPR language; whether to obtain a Plumb's content license or stay strictly label+literature; pricing model; AVMA/AAVSB positioning. I am not a lawyer — these need counsel.

## 6. Maintenance cycle

Monthly: PubMed scan on each KB drug and disease name (new RCTs, safety alerts, resistance reports); FDA CVM label changes and Dear Doctor letters; FARAD digest updates. Output: a dated `docs/lit_scan_YYYY-MM.md` with proposed KB diffs, nothing applied without approval.
Quarterly: full re-read of withdrawal strings against current labels; 21 CFR 530.41 list check; USDA reportable list check.
Annually: external veterinary reviewer pass on the whole KB using the dose_review workbook; version bump to next minor.
Every release: build → headless test (390 px, zero console errors) → VERSION bump → commit/push after summary approval.
