# Popular peptides — equine applicability screen (September 2026)

Purpose: decide which of the peptides currently marketed to the public deserve a card in SILENT MEDIC VET, and in which tier. Per KB rules, anything landing in Tier 3C is reference-only: no dose, no route, no source of supply is recorded, and the card carries the red band. Nothing below is a treatment recommendation.

Method: current "most popular peptide" listings from consumer and vendor sites were used only to establish what people are buying (sources at end); every clinical statement was then checked against PubMed for equine-specific data on 2026-09-06.

## Findings by peptide

Semaglutide / tirzepatide / retatrutide (GLP-1 class). Already in KB as Tier 3B (human-approved). Equine relevance is real but in the opposite direction from the human use case: in horses the incretin GLP-1 is implicated as a driver of the hyperinsulinaemia that causes laminitis. A GLP-1 receptor antagonist reduced the insulin response to a high-glycaemic meal in ponies with insulin dysregulation (Kemp, de Laat et al. J Anim Sci 2023, PMID 38066683, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jas/skad389)), while a single dose of the agonist exenatide lowered insulin AUC and raised estimated insulin sensitivity in a six-horse crossover pilot (Frank et al. BMC Vet Res 2022, PMID 35906619, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12917-022-03394-2)). The two results are not reconciled and neither is a treatment trial. Action: keep 3B; add an equine DVM note that the evidence is conflicting, limited to n=6, and that no GLP-1 agonist has any equine safety or laminitis-outcome data. Flag as FEI-controlled. No weight-loss use in horses.

BPC-157. In KB as 3C. PubMed search for equine data returned nothing; the evidence base remains rodent tissue-healing models. Prohibited by FEI and by WADA (S0 non-approved substances). Action: no change; the equine cell stays "no equine data; prohibited in competition".

TB-500 / thymosin β4. In KB as 3C. The only equine literature is doping-control work: TB4 is banned by IFHA, FEI and WADA, online products claiming to contain it are widespread, and a synthesis impurity is now detectable in equine plasma after a single administration (Drug Test Anal 2024, PMID 39314109, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/dta.3806)). Action: add this PMID to the card's regulatory note. No efficacy data in any veterinary species.

CJC-1295 / ipamorelin / sermorelin / tesamorelin (GHRH analogues and GH secretagogues). In KB as 3C. Equine literature is again doping detection only: an immuno-PCR screen (Drug Test Anal 2018, PMID 30489688) and an LC-MS/MS confirmation method for CJC-1295 in equine plasma (Drug Test Anal 2019, PMID 30938069). Action: cite both on the card; note that GH-axis stimulation in a species prone to insulin dysregulation is a plausible harm, not a benefit.

GHK-Cu. In KB as 3C. No equine data in PubMed. Topical wound-healing claims rest on in-vitro and rodent work. Action: no change.

MOTS-c, epitalon, NAD+ injections, "longevity" peptides. Grouped in KB as 3C. No equine data. Action: no change.

PT-141 (bremelanotide), KPV, selank/semax, AOD-9604, cagrilintide. Not in KB. No equine data found for any of them. Action: do not add individual cards; extend the existing "other unapproved peptides" 3C card with a one-line list so a search for the name returns the red-band card rather than nothing.

Bioactive collagen peptides. Not a "peptide" in the marketed sense; these are dietary hydrolysates. A 2026 two-part narrative review in Vet Sci covers veterinary trial data (PMID 42655766). Action: candidate for a Tier 4 nutraceutical card in v0.3 after reading the review in full; evidence grade will likely be B–C.

## What goes into the KB in v0.2

1. Semaglutide/GLP-1 card: equine DVM note with PMIDs 38066683 and 35906619; FEI flag.
2. TB-500 card: PMID 39314109 in regulatory note.
3. CJC-1295/ipamorelin card: PMIDs 30489688 and 30938069.
4. "Other unapproved peptides" card: name list extended (PT-141, KPV, selank, semax, AOD-9604, cagrilintide, sermorelin, tesamorelin).
5. Every equine cell on a 3C card: status "no", note "Prohibited under FEI/USEF rules; no equine safety or efficacy data."

No doses, routes, or suppliers are recorded for any 3C substance, consistent with the KB rule.
